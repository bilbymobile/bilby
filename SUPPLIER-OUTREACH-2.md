# Outreach: eSIM Access and CMLink

Sequencing: **eSIM Access first**, because there is no minimum order, no setup
fee, the adapter is already written, and you can be transacting this week.
**CMLink in parallel**, because it is a distributor scale conversation with a
longer lead time and may not clear the bar for a sole trader.

Do not mention the ad funded free tier to either. That product is parked, and
introducing it invites the same three deflections you already collected. You are
a paid travel eSIM reseller with an integration already built.

**Route.** Create the account first at `console.esimaccess.com`, then use the
sales form at `esimaccess.com/contact/`. There is no published email address on
their site. I checked the contact page directly in a browser and it carries a
form only.

**Form fields.** Name Nav. Company Nextwave, trading as Bilby Mobile. Country
Australia. Industry: **Loyal program** if you want consistency with the other
enquiries, or **Travel Tech** if you would rather be read as an app business.
Either is defensible.

## 2. CMLink, distributor track

**To:** `esim_distributor@cmlink.com`

Use the **distributor** address, not the partner one. Their page describes the
Service Provider track as being for operators who already hold eSIM inventory and
want CMLink to distribute it, which is the opposite of what you want. The
Dealer and Distributor track is for businesses selling eSIM products to
consumers. That is you.

## What counts as a good answer

**Local breakout available anywhere in Southeast Asia.** This is the one that
matters most and the one nobody offers. If either supplier can do it, pay more
for it and build the brand on it.

**A throttle number they will put in writing.** If they will not state it, you
cannot sell the plan under your own spec sheet, which makes them unusable
regardless of price.

**No top up ceiling, or a high one.** eSIM Access's documented limit of ten is a
structural problem for a top up rather than reissue product.

**A rate card before money.** Any supplier who wants a deposit before showing
prices is telling you something about the prices.

## What to do with a bad answer

If both come back with Hong Kong breakout only, that is genuinely useful. It
means the cheap Asia segment is structurally non local, and your differentiator
has to move from routing to something you can actually control: support, the
spec sheet honesty itself, and activation reliability. Those are still unclaimed
and still worth a brand.
