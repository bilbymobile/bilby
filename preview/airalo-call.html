Meeting card · Hang Tran, Head of Strategic Partnerships, Airalo

You are there for three numbers. Everything else is conversation.

Three support tickets could not produce them, which is exactly why this call exists. A partnerships lead can authorise things a catalogue cannot, so ask for the non standard arrangement openly rather than asking again about the standard one.

Your opening ninety seconds. "I run a travel eSIM app in Australia. The free tier is funded by advertising rather than by the user, which makes it an acquisition channel that costs me ad revenue instead of cash, and those users convert into exactly the paid plans you already sell well. The integration against your API is already written. What I need from this call is whether the commercial shape fits, because my redemption unit is 50 MB and your smallest catalogue top up is 3 GB."

That opening does three things: names the value to them, proves you are not a tyre kicker, and puts the hard number on the table in the first two minutes rather than the last five.

The three that decide it

01

Can you sell me a pooled balance I draw down in arbitrary megabytes across many ICCIDs?

If yes, nothing else on this page matters and you should spend the rest of the call on price. Pooled capacity is normal in wholesale and never appears on a partner page, because partner pages are written for people selling 1 GB bundles to tourists.

If they dodge "Is that a no for the standard platform, or a no for Airalo? I am asking partnerships because I know it is not in the catalogue."
02

What is the smallest top up you can create for a partner, as opposed to the smallest in the public catalogue?

Their own developer docs show the smallest documented top up as 3 GB at $8 net. You already know the catalogue answer, so do not ask for it. Ask whether a custom SKU can be cut, and at what size.

If they dodge "My redemption is 50 MB. At 3 GB I buy sixty times what I hand out. Is there any size below 500 MB available under any commercial arrangement?"
03

Do you sell a speed capped SKU, or expose per profile throughput control?

The whole free tier problem disappears at 256 or 512 kbps, because a slow lane is cheap and a slow user consumes slowly. This is how the closest comparable operator solved it, and no reseller publishes such a SKU anywhere.

If they dodge "Not an FUP that kicks in after a gigabyte. A bundle that is slow from the first byte, sold at a slow bundle price."

Commercial, once the three are answered

Oceania rate at volume. Australia switches on below $2.157 per GB and is dead above it. Do not ask for a discount, ask what volume reaches that number.
Minimum commitment, platform fee, setup fee. eSIM Go wants $1,000 a month. Find out where Airalo sits before you fall in love.
Rate card for Indonesia, Thailand, Japan, Vietnam, Pakistan and China, at your volumes.
Failed activation. Support said case by case. Ask who absorbs the cost in practice, because it decides whether support is an annoyance or a line item.
Is anyone else on the platform doing ad funded? They have thousands of partners. Somebody has tried it, and what happened is worth more than any rate.

Test in sandbox before you sign anything

Top up ceiling. A competing platform caps an eSIM at ten top ups, ever. A drip fed free tier is nothing but top ups. Ask whether Airalo has an equivalent limit, then verify it.
Recycle window. Support said 90 days to activate and 90 days grace from package expiry, restartable by a top up. Confirm it in writing and test that a top up genuinely restarts the clock.
Provisioning latency. Order to installable profile. A user standing at an airport gate will not wait ninety seconds.
Usage reporting granularity and lag. You cannot run a metered free tier if consumption data arrives hours late.

Read the table honestly. The best published rate anywhere on earth is Simbase in Europe at twenty five cents, and that is still more than double what a redemption can bear. Outside Europe nothing comes within a factor of nine. No supplier's published price makes the metered model work, so the number to listen for from Airalo is not a rate at all. Now do the same sum for the throttled model. A thirty minute session at 0.21 Mbps has a ceiling of 47 MB, and real messaging use lands nearer 5 MB. At an ordinary bulk rate of $2.20 per GB that session costs $0.011, against the $0.0108 one ad view generates. It breaks even on the first ad and improves with every bulk discount. That is the whole argument, in two lines, and it is why the third question on this page matters more than the second.

Precedent, and the lack of it

No visible precedent on their platform. Airalo's partner content is entirely about selling packages through new channels, not about fragmenting them. Their one named loyalty partner is United Airlines MileagePlus, and what United gets is discounted standard data plans as a frequent flyer perk. That is placement, not granularity.
So you are asking for a first. Not impossible, and a partnerships lead is the right person to ask, but go in knowing there is no case study they can point at. Expect "let me check internally" and make the follow up date explicit before you hang up.
They do court redemption partners. Airalo publishes a steady stream on loyalty and rewards programmes, so the framing is one they already sell into. Use their language: yours is a rewards redemption model where the currency happens to be earned by watching a video.
Somebody is already doing your model. Airvoy, from Routed Labs in Delaware, runs a free travel eSIM tier earned by completing short tasks across 190 countries. Worth knowing they exist, and worth noting what they chose: a capped speed tier renewable indefinitely, not metered megabytes. That is the third independent operator to reach the same conclusion.

Do not reveal

The pricing engine is the only thing you own

  • Describe the product, that ads fund small data grants. Do not describe the three country model, how home, current location and destination are blended, or the retention constant.
  • No margin structure, no contribution figures, no cost per MB assumptions. You just locked those behind a password for good reason.
  • Volume: give the budget bounded numbers below and nothing more. A forecast offered casually becomes a minimum commitment in a contract.
  • If they ask for the app or a demo, show the app. Do not send code, and do not walk them through the pricing logic.

Lead with what they get

Airalo has supply and no consumer growth engine in Australia. You are building an acquisition channel whose free tier costs you advertising revenue rather than cash, and whose users convert into exactly the paid plans Airalo already sells well.

That is the trade, and it is worth saying in the first two minutes. A partnerships lead is measured on partners who bring volume, not on partners who need favours.

Before the call

Finish the partner signup and get catalogue access. This is the single highest value hour of prep. With the catalogue open you stop asking what the smallest top up is and start saying "I can see 3 GB is my floor, can you cut smaller", which is a different conversation with a different power balance.
Have the app installed on your phone, ready to show. Not the code.
Have this page open on a second screen.

After the call, same day

Send a written summary of what was agreed. Verbal rate commitments evaporate.
Ask for sandbox credentials in that same email, while goodwill is fresh.
Book the equivalent call with eSIM Access and eSIM Go this week. One quote is not a negotiation.
If pooled or sub 500 MB is a firm no, say so plainly in the notes. It closes the metered model and the timed model becomes the plan.

On recording: most Australian states require the consent of every party before a private conversation is recorded. If you want a recording, say so at the start and get a yes on the call. Otherwise take notes.
