Decision · after the supplier research

Start by selling, not by giving away.

Everything learned in the last week points at one conclusion, and it inverts the plan. The free tier is the hardest thing in this business and it is currently scheduled first.

Launch Bilby as an honest paid travel eSIM, with no free tier at all. Sign with Airalo for supply, because they are courting you, there is no minimum commitment, and their 3 GB packages are exactly right for someone buying a plan.

Then turn advertising on afterwards, capped at five dollars a day, and let it tell you what an ad view is really worth. That single measurement re-prices the entire model, and you cannot obtain it any other way.

Why, in four findings

01

The metered model cannot be bought at any price

A 50 MB redemption can cost eleven cents. The cheapest published rate on earth is Simbase in Europe at twenty five, and outside Europe nothing comes within a factor of nine. Airalo's floor forces an eight dollar purchase to hand over fifty megabytes. This is not a negotiation problem, it is what the market charges for granularity.

02

Everyone who solved this sells time, not megabytes

Firsty sells thirty minute sessions at 0.21 Mbps. Airvoy sells a 512 kbps tier. Both bound their cost with a throttle rather than with procurement. At an ordinary bulk rate a five megabyte session costs $0.011 against the $0.0108 an ad generates, so the throttled model closes at prices anyone will sell you today.

03

Your free tier is roughly ten times too generous

Airvoy, live today, gives about 0.7 MB per ad view and throttles it. Bilby's model grants 5 to 13 MB at full speed. They are shipping and you are not, so the burden of proof sits with your numbers, not theirs.

04

Publishing is what unlocks the measurement

AdMob will not issue a live ad unit for an unpublished app, so the real eCPM, the real fill rate and the real redemption behaviour are all invisible until Bilby is on the store. You cannot validate the free tier before launching, which means you must launch something that works without it. That is not a compromise, it is the only order the dependencies allow.

Phase 2 · Turn ads on, capped hard

Once published

Now AdMob will serve, and the one number nobody can give you becomes measurable. Keep the free tier deliberately small and honest, and let the daily budget be the safety rail it was built to be.

  • Set the daily budget to $5. Maximum exposure is $150 a month, and the app already tells users plainly when the pool is spent.
  • Open the free tier in one or two cheap destinations only, not everywhere.
  • Measure the real eCPM, fill rate, ads per user per day and redemption rate in week one. The 0.4 retention constant in the pricing engine is a guess until this runs.
  • Accept the overbuy on every redemption. You are paying for information, and $150 is cheap for it.
Worst case $150 / monthBuys the number that re-prices everything

Phase 3 · Choose the architecture on evidence

Month 3 onward

With real revenue per view and real usage, throttled against metered stops being a debate and becomes arithmetic. By then you also have a trading history, which is the thing every rate desk actually wants.

  • Take the measured eCPM to Sparks and emnify, who both sell the throttle.
  • Finish whichever branch of the entitlement abstraction the data selects, and delete the other.
  • Revisit Australia. It needs $2.157 per GB and the going rate is $2.20, and volume is what closes two percent.

Stop doing

Three things that are costing time and returning nothing

  • Stop asking anyone for a 50 MB SKU. Four requests, four deflections, and the answer was in Airalo's public developer docs the whole time. It does not exist at a price that works, anywhere.
  • Stop treating the free tier as the launch. It is the second product, it depends on a supplier you have not found and a measurement you cannot take yet, and holding the launch for it delays the paid business that already works.
  • Stop competing on price per gigabyte. Firsty retails at about $1.60 per GB, below the wholesale you are modelling. That fight is unwinnable with reseller economics and it is not where your advantage is.

The one that can still end it

Regulation, and it is now urgent rather than eventual

Reselling mobile connectivity under your own brand appears to make you a Carriage Service Provider in Australia. TIO membership becomes mandatory, the TCP Code applies and is enforceable by ACMA, and the Telecommunications Amendment (Enhancing Consumer Safeguards) Bill 2025 introduces a registration scheme under which wholesale providers are prohibited from supplying an unregistered CSP.

Read that last part again. A supplier may be legally unable to sell to you until you are registered, which puts the lawyer ahead of the contract rather than after it. Maximum penalties reach roughly ten million dollars.

This is not legal advice and I am not a lawyer. It is worth one paid hour with an Australian telecommunications lawyer before you sign anything, and that hour is the cheapest risk reduction available to you.
