Gate two

The money path

built and verified

The pipeline from a person choosing a plan to a working eSIM in their phone. All of it is written and tested except the one piece that needs something only the supplier can give you.

The three properties worth naming

Eight concurrent fulfilments of one paid order call the supplier exactly once and grant exactly one entitlement. A redelivered Stripe webhook does not pay the same order twice. A request without a valid signature cannot mark anything paid and never reaches the supplier.

Nothing retries by itself, deliberately. A timeout against a supplier is indistinguishable from a success you did not hear about, so an automatic retry is how one payment buys two profiles. Retry is a button, pressed by somebody who has checked the supplier's own order list first.

Gate four

Production basics

two done, four small

This gate came out of auditing the API surface rather than listing it from memory. The audit found two things that were not thin or partial but entirely absent. Both are now built.

What the audit found, and what it cost to fix

Nothing reported a failed fulfilment. If a supplier call threw at two in the morning, the customer saw an error and you found out when they emailed you, if they emailed you. For a product whose entire claim is that somebody answers, discovering your own failures second is the wrong way round.

And no endpoint had a rate limit, including the one that spends supplier money. A new store with card payments attracts card testing within days of going live, and the cost of that is not the fraud, it is the dispute fees and the Stripe risk review that follows.

Both are done, and getting the error grouping right took two attempts, both of which produced a function that looked correct and did nothing. Fifty failures differing only in an order id produced fifty groups, because an underscore is a word character and there is therefore no word boundary between it and the digits after it. Neither attempt would have been caught by reading the code.
