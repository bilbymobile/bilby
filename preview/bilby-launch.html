Two things to fix, and one trap

bilbymobile.nextwave.au is pointing at the wrong place. It resolves to a pair of addresses that are not the ones your project answers on, and nothing responds when you connect. Repoint that CNAME at the project value above.

status.bilbymobile.com should not point at Vercel. A status page hosted on the same platform as the thing it reports on goes down with it, which is the one moment it exists for. Cloudflare Pages or GitHub Pages are both free and independent. Until you pick one, leave the record off rather than pointing it at the same deployment.

The trap: DNS is only half of it. Each hostname also has to be added to the project in Vercel under Settings, Domains. A domain with perfect DNS that has not been attached returns a deployment not found page, which looks exactly like a DNS problem and is not one.

app-ads.txt is the one that fails silently

It must sit at the root of bilbymobile.com and resolve with no redirect. If it does not, AdMob puts the app into limited serving and the console never tells you why, the revenue is simply lower forever. That is why the apex serves directly and www redirects to it rather than the other way round.

The three monetisation switches

You asked for all three and to decide later, so all three get built behind flags that default to off. Turning one on is a setting in the staff console, not a deploy.

SwitchWhat it doesMy honest read
Google Ads
buy traffic		 Conversion tracking on purchase, so you can see cost per sale rather than cost per click. The one worth turning on first. Build the tracking now even at zero budget, because retrofitting conversion data loses you the history you need to judge the first campaign.
AdSense
sell space		 Display slots on the marketing pages only. Never on checkout, never in the web app. Off by default and I would leave it off. On a page where someone is about to spend twenty dollars, an ad unit earns you fractions of a cent and can hand your buyer to a competitor. If you do switch it on, put it on future blog and guide pages, not the landing page.
AdMob
rewarded video		 Rewarded video in the Android app, wired to the credit ledger that already exists. Worth wiring even with the free tier off, because the SDK, the app-ads.txt crawl and the Play declarations all have lead time. Switching it on later then costs nothing.

Many suppliers, and room for what comes after eSIMs

You asked for something you can extend, so the design separates what you sell from who delivers it. A catalogue item is a thing with a price and a description. A fulfiller is whoever actually provisions it. An eSIM plan happens to be a catalogue item fulfilled by a wholesale eSIM provider, and nothing in that sentence is specific to eSIMs, which is what makes a second product category cheap later.

interface Fulfiller {
  readonly id: string;              // "esimaccess", "airalo", "resellportal"
  readonly kind: "esim" | string;   // the category, so a future product is not a special case
  health(): Promise<Health>;        // polled, and shown in the console
  balance(): Promise<Money | null>; // prepaid wallet, when the provider has one
  quote(item: CatalogItem): Promise<Quote>;
  fulfil(order: Order): Promise<Fulfilment>;
}

Three things make this work rather than just look tidy:

Routing lives in the database, not in code. A row per country saying preferred provider and fallback provider. Changing who serves Indonesia is a click in the console, not a deploy, which matters the first time a provider has an outage on a Friday night.

Cost is captured on the order, never looked up. The rate you paid on the day is a fact about that order. Recomputing margin from today's rate card makes last month's numbers move, and a dashboard whose history changes is one nobody trusts twice.

A provider that fails health checks stops receiving orders automatically and the console says so, rather than silently failing customer purchases until someone notices.

Running alongside, on your clock not mine

  1. Carriage service provider registrationWholesale providers are prohibited from supplying an unregistered CSP under the 2025 amendments. This gates supply itself, so it sits ahead of every commercial conversation, and it is the single item most likely to delay launch.
  2. Stripe underwritingElevated risk category, weeks of lead time, and a real chance of decline. Apply in week one so a no arrives while there is still time to arrange an alternative.
  3. GST, now that you are the merchantStripe is not a merchant of record, so the tax is yours. Registration is required once turnover passes the threshold, sales to Australian consumers carry GST, and every tax invoice must show the ABN even though no page prints it. Worth an hour with an accountant before the first sale rather than after the first quarter.
  4. The telecommunications lawyerStill unbooked. Written only support under the TCP Code, comparative advertising if you name competitors, and the CSP obligations all land in the same conversation.
  5. The coverage claims190 plus countries and 200 plus carriers are representations about a future matter until a supplier is signed. Either evidence them or change them before the page is public.
