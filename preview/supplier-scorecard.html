What is already known, before anyone replies

eSIM Access — no minimum order, no setup fee, adapter already written. But their own docs cap an eSIM at ten top ups, ever, which scores 1 on that row unless they say otherwise.
CMLink — the source behind several cheap Australian brands, but the plans resold from them advertise Hong Kong and Singapore IP addresses, so expect 1 on breakout unless they surprise you.
Airalo — smallest documented top up is 3 GB at $8 net, best documented platform in the category, courting you already. Strong on reliability, weak on granularity.
ResellPortal — $0 fees and a $20 deposit, so cheap to learn on. Published wholesale is $0.70 for a 1 GB 7 day plan, roughly A$1.07 per GB, and their pricing page mentions no sandbox.

One thing the score cannot capture. Ookla measured travel eSIM latency in Japan at 54 ms with local routing against 543 ms when traffic exited through Madrid, with worst cases thirty times higher than local users on the same network. Their conclusion was that true local breakout remains uncommon.

So if every supplier scores 1 on breakout, that is not four failures. It is the market telling you that routing is not where a small operator can differentiate, and the answer moves to the things you control: support that answers, activation that works, and publishing the numbers everyone else hides.
