# "Bilby" — Australian trade mark knockout search

**Searched:** 14 August 2026
**Where:** [Australian Trade Mark Search](https://search.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademarks), IP Australia's own register
**Query:** word `bilby` (part match), class 38 with associated classes — returns classes 9, 37, 38, 41, 42
**Result set:** 34 marks

> **This is a knockout search, not legal clearance.** IP Australia's own
> disclaimer says results should not be relied upon, because any application
> you file is re-examined by an examiner who may find material this search
> did not. It tells you whether to stop — it cannot tell you it is safe to go.

---

## Headline

**Class 38 (telecommunications) is completely clear.** Zero marks containing
"bilby" — nothing registered, nothing pending, nothing lapsed. For a business
whose core service is telecommunications, that is the single most important
result and it is as clean as it gets.

**Class 42 (software and technology services) has three live registrations**,
none of them in telecom.

**Class 9 (which covers mobile apps) has three registered "BILBY" word marks.**
This is the one that would have been missed by searching only 38 and 42 — and
it is the class your Android app most plausibly falls into. All three turn out
to be in unrelated fields, but you should know they exist.

## The rest of the register

The remaining 22 results are overwhelmingly **class 41** (education and
entertainment) and cluster around Easter Bilby / conservation / children's
characters — EASTER BILBY, WILBY BILBY, SILBY BILBY, BANJO BILBY, Bondi Bilby,
Charleville Bilby Experience. Most are lapsed or removed. None are near your
trade.

That cluster is worth seeing for a different reason: it confirms the bilby is
strongly associated with **conservation and children's brands** in Australia.
That is the context your brand will land in. It supports the conservation
angle; it also means your visual identity should not read as a children's
character.

## What I'd do

1. **File in class 38 first.** It is clear, it is your actual service, and it is
   the strongest position you can hold.
2. **Decide on class 9 with a lawyer.** Your app is arguably class 9 software,
   and that is where the three registered BILBY marks sit. A trade mark
   attorney can tell you in one call whether to file, file narrowly, or rely on
   38 alone.
3. **Consider class 42** if you ever sell the platform as software.
4. **Do not treat this as clearance.** No search of the register catches
   unregistered common-law rights, business names, or a similar mark that is
   spelled differently. That is what an attorney's clearance search is for, and
   it costs a few hundred dollars against a brand you are about to spend months
   building.

## Bottom line

Nothing here says stop. The most important class for your business is
completely empty, and every close mark sits in an unrelated trade. Class 9 is
the one to get advice on, and it is a conversation rather than a wall.
