# Brand kit

Everything here is driven by `web/src/lib/brand.ts`. Change the name, slug,
domain and package there, re-run `python3 brand/make-icons.py`, and the product
and icon set follow.

---

## 1. The name

**Decided: Bilby.** The shortlist it came from is in [NAMES.md](NAMES.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| Meaning | The greater bilby — an endangered desert marsupial with outsized ears. Australia's answer to the Easter bunny. |
| Sound | BIL-bee. Two soft syllables. Anyone in the world can say it and spell it, which Dinkum and Cooee could not claim. |
| Not travel-locked | Says nothing about roaming, so it carries into a domestic product without a word changing. That was the brief. |
| Gives you a mark | The ears are the logo, and they double as an antenna. A slogan-name like "Dinkum" gives you a wordmark and nothing to draw. |
| Collision | No telco or eSIM brand using it. Nothing near Airalo, Holafly, Saily, Firsty, Nomad. |
| Watch | Conservation groups use the bilby heavily (Save the Bilby Fund, Haigh's Easter Bilby). That is a partnership opportunity as much as a risk — see below. |

### The conservation angle is worth taking seriously

The bilby is endangered, and the whole product position is *being straight with
people*. A published commitment — a fixed share of paid revenue to bilby
conservation, reported honestly — is the rare brand story that is both true and
strategically useful. It also pre-empts the "you're just using an endangered
animal as a mascot" reading, which will otherwise come up.

Do it properly or not at all: a real agreement, a real number, a public figure
you update. A vague "we care about wildlife" line is worse than silence.

### Before you commit

1. **IP Australia trade mark search** (free) — classes 38 (telecoms) and 42
   (software). A name that collides is worth abandoning now, not after you have
   ten thousand installs.
2. **Registrar check** on the actual domain. DNS resolution only tells you a
   name is *in use*; a domain with no record may still be registered and parked.
3. **Play Store + App Store search** for the exact string.
4. **Say it to five people** over a bad line and ask them to spell it back.

Nothing is blocked on this decision — `web/src/lib/brand.ts` and
`app/lib/brand.dart` are one-line changes, and `make-icons.py` regenerates every
asset.

## 2. The mark

Two ears above a simple head.

The ears do double duty: an animal with famously oversized ears, and an antenna
picking up signal. Both readings land without explanation, which is what keeps
it from being an animal mascot with nothing to do with the product.

Three elements only, and deliberately so. Two earlier marks were discarded — a
symmetrical chevron that looked like every VPN icon on the store, and a wing
with wake arcs that turned to mush at 48dp. **The launcher icon is the only
brand impression most users consciously receive, and it is 48dp** — legibility
there outranks richness at 512 every time.

The glyph is auto-centred by its alpha bounding box rather than hand-tuned
offsets, because the ears are asymmetric and tilted, so the centre of the
drawing instructions is not the optical centre of the result.

Generated by `make-icons.py`, which emits:

```
assets/play/icon-512.png                    Play Store icon (exact spec)
assets/play/feature-graphic-1024x500.png    Play feature graphic
assets/web/icon-{192,512}.png               PWA / manifest
assets/web/apple-touch-icon.png             iOS home screen
assets/android/mipmap-*/ic_launcher*.png    Legacy + adaptive launcher icons
```

Adaptive foregrounds are drawn at 44% coverage because Android crops to a circle
and applies parallax; anything larger clips the ear tips on some launchers.

**Still to do by hand:** the feature graphic ships without text. Add the
wordmark and tagline in your own tool — Play rejects graphics where text is
clipped by the safe area on small screens, and that is a judgement call a script
should not make.

## 3. Colour

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `bg` | `#07090D` | Page background |
| `surface` | `#0E1319` | Cards |
| `surface2` | `#151C25` | Inset elements, meters |
| `border` | `#1E2833` | Hairlines |
| `text` | `#E8EEF5` | Primary text |
| `muted` | `#8B9AAB` | Secondary text |
| `accent` | `#2EE6A8` | The brand. Actions, balances, "connected" |
| `accentDim` | `#10A97A` | Gradient partner, the second ear |
| `warn` | `#FFB347` | Caps reached, budget exhausted |
| `danger` | `#FF6B6B` | Failures, negative contribution |

Dark-first, deliberately. This is an app opened in an airport at 11pm, on a
phone at 8% battery, by someone who has just got off a plane.

`accent` on `bg` gives a contrast ratio around 11:1 — comfortably past WCAG AA
for both text and UI components. Do not put `accent` on `surface2` for body
text; it passes, but only just.

## 4. Voice

**Is:** plain, specific, unhurried, honest about limits.
**Is not:** breathless, salesy, emoji-laden, vague about what "free" means.

An ad-funded free tier lives or dies on trust. The moment the copy sounds like
it is hiding something, users assume the free data is bait.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Unlimited free data!" | "6 MB per ad, up to 10 ads a day. Enough for maps and messages." |
| "Oops! Something went wrong." | "The ad didn't finish, so nothing was credited." |
| "Upgrade now to unlock!" | "Full speed and hotspot, $1.50 for the day." |
| "You've reached your limit." | "That's today's 10 ads. Resets at 00:00 UTC." |

The app already says *"Free data isn't offered in AU — wholesale rates here cost
more than an ad view earns, and we'd rather say so than quietly hand you 2 MB."*
That sentence is the voice. Nobody else in this category talks like that, and
it is the cheapest trust you will ever buy.

## 5. Play Store listing

**App name (30 char max)**
```
Bilby — Free eSIM Data
```

**Short description (80 char max)**
```
Free data abroad. Watch an ad, get online. Pay only for the days you need speed.
```

**Full description (4000 char max)**
```
Big ears. Free data.

Bilby is a travel eSIM with a free tier that actually stays free. Install
one eSIM before you fly. When you land, watch a short ad and get data credited
straight to it — enough for maps, messages, and calling a ride. No SIM swap,
no roaming bill, no plastic.

WHAT'S FREE
• Data credited for every ad you watch, up to 10 a day
• Enough for Maps, WhatsApp, iMessage, email and rideshare apps
• Your eSIM installs once and stays on your phone — future top-ups are silent

WHEN YOU WANT MORE
• Day passes from around $1.50 for full-speed data
• Trip passes from 3 GB to 10 GB
• Hotspot sharing on paid plans
• Buy only for the days you actually need it

HOW MUCH FREE DATA YOU GET
The amount changes by destination, and we're upfront about why: data costs us
more in some countries than others. You'll get more in Bangkok than in Suva,
and we'll tell you exactly how much before you watch anything.

In a few countries the numbers don't work and we don't offer a free tier at
all — rather than quietly hand you 2 MB and call it free.

WORKS IN 190+ COUNTRIES
Automatic connection to a local network wherever you land. No contracts, no
account required to start.

WHAT YOU NEED
• An eSIM-capable, carrier-unlocked phone
• Android 10 or later for one-tap eSIM install
• Wi-Fi when you first install your eSIM

A NOTE ON ROAMING
Your Bilby eSIM is a roaming profile. After installing, switch data roaming
ON for it in your phone's settings — otherwise it won't connect. Keep your home
SIM for calls and texts, and set Bilby as your data line.
```

**Category:** Travel & Local
**Content rating:** Everyone
**Contains ads:** Yes — declare it. Not declaring ads is a removable offence.
**In-app purchases:** Yes, if you sell via Play Billing. If you keep checkout on
the web, declare accurately and be prepared to explain the model in review.

### Screenshots you need

Minimum 2, up to 8, at least 1080px on the short side. In priority order:

1. The earn screen with a real balance — this is the whole pitch
2. The destination table showing different grants per country — nobody else has this
3. One-tap install screen
4. Plans list
5. "Free data isn't offered here" — proof of honesty, and it converts

Play Console's device-frame tool is adequate. Do not use marketing mockups that
don't match the app; review flags mismatches.
