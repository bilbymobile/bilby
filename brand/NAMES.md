# Naming shortlist

> **DECIDED: Bilby.** Domain `bilbymobile.com` — `bilby.com` is held by a
> squatter (which my DNS check could not see: it had no DNS record but was
> still registered, exactly the limitation flagged below). The shortlist below
> is kept as the record of what was considered and why.

**Brief:** Australian in flavour. Punchy, easy to say. **Not locked to travel or
roaming** — the name has to survive a domestic product later.

## How to read the domain column

I tested by DNS resolution. **"free?" only means the domain has no DNS record —
it may still be registered and parked.** It is a filter for obvious dead ends,
not proof of availability. Confirm at a registrar before committing to anything.

Every candidate below still needs an **IP Australia trade mark search in classes
38 (telecommunications) and 42 (software)** before you spend a dollar on it.

actually means. Names here say that directly, and they carry into a domestic
product without a word changing.

# Domain: the Bilby options

`bilby.com` is registered and parked by a squatter. My DNS check could not see
that — it had no DNS record, which is exactly the limitation flagged at the top
of this document. Treat that as the lesson: **DNS resolution filters dead ends,
it does not prove availability.**

Every `bilby.<tld>` that consumers would actually recognise is taken:
`.app`, `.co`, `.io`, `.me`, `.net`, `.org`, `.com.au`, `.net.au`, `.dev`.

So the choice is a combination. All of these showed no DNS record, which — as
above — means *worth checking at a registrar*, not *free*.

| Option | Reads as | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **bilbymobile.com** | Bilby Mobile | **Recommended.** "X Mobile" is the established Australian carrier convention — felix mobile, Boost Mobile. Signals a real telco, not an app. Critically, it works for a **domestic** product, which was your brief |
| **bilbydata.com** | Bilby Data | Sharpest fit for what you actually sell — data, not voice. Slightly narrows you if you ever add calls or a number |
| **bilby.network** | — | Semantically perfect, but `.network` is unfamiliar to consumers and looks like infrastructure, not a brand |
| **heybilby.com** | Hey Bilby | Friendly, but the "hey" prefix is a 2020s startup tic that will date |
| **trybilby.com** | Try Bilby | "Try" signals a trial rather than a company. Fine for a campaign page, wrong for a home |
| **getbilby.app** | Get Bilby | Same problem, plus a verb you have to explain |
| **bilby.travel** | — | Travel-locked. You explicitly said the brand must work at home too — this fences you out |

## Recommendation

**Buy `bilbymobile.com` as the primary.** Also grab `bilbydata.com`,
`bilbymobile.com.au` and `getbilby.com` defensively — domains are roughly $15
a year each and the cost of *not* holding them is someone else doing what the
`bilby.com` holder did to you.

The app is **"Bilby"** everywhere a user sees it — store listing, launcher, in
the product. "Bilby Mobile" is only the domain and the trading name. Nobody
types your domain anyway; they tap an install button.

Already applied in code:

```
brand.domain          bilbymobile.com
brand.androidPackage  com.bilbymobile.app
support email         hello@bilbymobile.com
```

**Do not publish to Play until the package id is final** — `androidPackage` is
immutable once an app is live. Changing it later means a new listing, zero
installs, zero reviews, and starting over.

## On bilby.com

Don't rule it out forever. Squatters sell. A domain broker enquiry costs nothing
and parked single-word `.com`s in a non-obvious niche often go for four figures
rather than six. Worth a message once you have revenue — but not worth delaying
launch by a single day.

## Skip the bundle

The bundle is `.com.au`, `.net`, `.info`, `.store`, `.online` for $34.67.
Only one of those five is worth anything to you.

- `.net` — marginal defensive value
- `.info` — spam-associated, actively unhelpful for a brand asking to be trusted
- `.store` — you are not a store
- `.online` — no signal at all

The pricing tells the story: **$321.63 struck through, $34.67 "for first year."**
Year two is the real number. That is roughly $290/yr to defend against a
squatting risk that does not exist — nobody speculatively registers
"bilbymobile.info".

Buy `.com.au` on its own instead.

## The `.com.au` conditions — read before you buy

`.com.au` is not open registration. auDA requires:

1. **Australian presence** — an active ABN or ACN. You have this via Nextwave.
2. **A name connection** — the domain must match your exact business or company
   name, an acronym of it, or the goods and services you provide.

If your ABN is registered to "Nextwave" and not "Bilby Mobile", register
**"Bilby Mobile" as a business name with ASIC** (about $44 for three years).
That makes eligibility unambiguous rather than an argument you have to win in
an audit.

**Eligibility is ongoing and auditable.** If the ABN is cancelled or the
business name lapses, the domain can be suspended or cancelled. This is not a
buy-and-forget asset like a `.com`.

Also consider **`bilbymobile.au`** — direct `.au` registration, shorter, and the
naming rules are more flexible than `.com.au`.

## Renewal is where registrars make their money

| Registrar | .com renewal | 5-year total |
|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | ~$10.44/yr | ~$52 |
| Porkbun | ~$10.99/yr | ~$55 |
| Namecheap | ~$13.98/yr | ~$62 |
| GoDaddy | ~$22.99/yr | ~$93 |

Take the cheap first year, then **transfer to Cloudflare or Porkbun** after the
60-day ICANN transfer lock. An inbound transfer adds a year to your existing
expiry, so you lose nothing. That is roughly $12/yr saved, forever, for ten
minutes of work.

`.com.au` cannot go to Cloudflare — use an Australian registrar such as
VentraIP or Netregistry.

## Do this first, before spending anything

**Free IP Australia trade mark search, classes 38 (telecommunications) and 42
(software).** Twenty minutes. If an existing "Bilby" mark sits in class 38 you
need to know now — $35 of domains is cheap to lose, six months of brand
equity is not.

## Verdict

Register `bilbymobile.com` today. Add `bilbymobile.com.au` once the ASIC
business name is sorted — which you need anyway for the telco compliance work.
Skip everything else. You can add domains at any time; the only thing you can
never get back is a name someone else took, and nobody is racing you for
`bilbymobile.store`.
